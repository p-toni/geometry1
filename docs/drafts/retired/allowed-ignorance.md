## Cut

The world does not arrive at the resolution I can keep. It arrives dense — too many differences at once, too many branches, too many live variables competing for the same small loop. So before I ever say I understand, something harsher has already happened.

I have removed material. Not because I am certain. Because I am bounded.

And a cut is not merely omission. It is **equivalence-making**: it declares that several visible differences can now be treated as one usable thing. That is the primitive act underneath compression — not removing detail, *licensing a collapse.*

Some differences are only surface. Some are only changes of coordinate. Some are the beginning of a new object. In the moment, all three feel equally expensive.

## Face

After the first cut I usually do not have the whole thing. I have a face: a visible side, a stable angle, a version of the object that feels coherent because I am still looking from the same direction.

And it does feel coherent. That is the problem.

From inside, a face and a form are nearly indistinguishable. Both give me something to hold. Both let me speak with confidence. The difference only shows up when I move — and I may not move for a long time.

> [aside|face]
> A clean explanation can just be a well-lit face. A good summary can just be a flattering projection. A framework can feel complete because it hides the seam from where I happen to be standing.

:::contrast face | form
- a visible, stable side | the whole object
- coherent from where I stand | keeps contact when turned
- a well-lit explanation | generates consequences
:::

## Rotation

What I wanted from geometry was never elegance. I wanted something that survives rotation.

If the wording changes, does the object remain? If the example changes? If the source closes, can I rebuild it? If the context shifts, do I still know what was structural and what was local scenery?

I do not understand a thing when I can repeat the same face. I understand it when I can turn it and keep contact.

[plate|PLATE II]
*The same form under rotation — if it disappears when turned, I only owned a face.* `/images/pieces/allowed-ignorance/plate-rotation.svg`

Read that way, the old tests are not tests of confidence. They are tests of whether the cut was faithful.

1. **Rephrase** — did the surface change while the form stayed?
2. **Rebuild** — is the structure mine without the source?
3. **Transfer** — did I keep the object, or only the setting it first appeared in?
4. **Predict** — does the form generate consequences instead of summaries?
5. **Break** — which cut removed something load-bearing?
6. **Relax** — does the map still orient after I weaken one of its claims?

## Void

A map is not the world made portable. It is the world with pieces missing, and the absence is not a defect — it is what makes the map usable.

This is where relief misleads me. The lighter object feels better in the hand. So I start treating the removed material as if it had never mattered.

On 28 July I found out what that costs.

The site had two essay readers. The one I kept handled seven block types. The pool uses eleven. `diagram`, `ladder`, `pull` and `edge-taxonomy` — **32 blocks across 9 of the 10 essays** — rendered as nothing at all.

Not badly. Not degraded. *Nothing.* For weeks I had been reading my own essays in a reader that was quietly deleting about a third of their structure, and I did not notice, because absence has no rendering. A missing paragraph leaves no gap you can see. The prose closes over it and reads fine.

> [thesis|the void is not inert]
> The removed material does not sit still. The void is exactly where a difference I stopped paying for waits to return as a fracture.

The four types all had a shape the system already defined. Nothing was hard. The cut had been made months earlier — *the reader handles the blocks that matter* — and it was an equivalence, and it was wrong, and it was invisible from every angle I had been standing at.

## Crack

Failure often looks less like chaos than like the return of a difference I stopped paying for.

[fig|late-failure-motif]

A neat cut is seductive precisely because it deletes. It lowers the cost of thought, it gives me something to stand inside, it makes the domain feel solved enough to move in. But reality does not care whether the cut felt good, and one small movement later the structure cracks along a line I had decided was cosmetic.

That is why the dangerous map is not always the loose one. It is often the elegant one. The one that failed late.

[plate|PLATE III]
*A crack reveals the difference the map stopped paying for.* `/images/pieces/allowed-ignorance/plate-crack.svg`

The fix was not better judgement. It was a test that now asserts every block type any essay actually uses survives the recast, so the reader cannot silently drop content again. A cut I cannot see is not disciplined by being more careful next time. It is disciplined by building something that shouts when the void grows.

So [[Weak Geometry|weak-geometry]] reads differently to me now. Leave some seams visible. Leave some cuts provisional. Do not merge faster than reality has licensed. Not because I want vagueness — because I want contact.

Understanding is not possession, and not even compactness. **It is a disciplined right to leave things out.** To learn is to get better at subtraction without losing the form; to think well is to know which variations are cheap to ignore, which are dangerous to ignore, and which only reveal their importance when the object is turned.

That may be all a bounded mind gets. Not the whole thing, not final fidelity — only a better and better sense for what it is allowed to leave out, plus an alarm for the times that sense is wrong.

The alarm is the new part. I did not have one in June, and 32 blocks went missing while I admired the cut.
