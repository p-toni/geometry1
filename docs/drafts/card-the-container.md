# Cards — the container

Instruments belonging to *the container*. These are not essay prose. They are the
objects the essay leaves behind, and they are here because the story could not
carry them without breaking.

---

## The private metric

*Does a finished piece deserve the slot it took?*

The bar: **the piece is good if it reduces future compute.** Not if it is admired.
Not if it is finished. If future-me can re-enter the space faster because it exists.

After writing something, check five things.

1. **Decision speed** — do I make faster calls in the same domain?
2. **Compression** — can I explain the core tension to myself in fewer steps?
3. **Reuse** — do I reach for the concept unprompted?
4. **Re-entry** — is there less startup cost when I return to the problem?
5. **Contact** — does it change what I test, build, avoid, or notice next?

If yes, the piece increased extractable structure.

If no, it is just text.

**The failure this catches:** the pieces I have been proudest of were frequently the
ones with the highest re-entry cost, because polish and re-entry are not the same
axis and I kept scoring one as the other. A clean draft I cannot get back inside
did not stabilise anything. It only performed.

*Unchanged since January 2026 — the only part of this argument I have never had to revise.*

## The standing orders

The dials, not the wall. Always present, no ceremony.

> Build geometry.
> Reduce leakage.
> Stay in the convergent basin.
> Do not harden the map faster than evidence can carry.

## The ten-minute run

*When the week has already gone wrong — later than I should, still earlier than the
alternative.*

1. **List what is live.** Not what is planned. Not what matters. What is taking a
   slot, including the things that are only waiting.
2. **Find the phantoms.** Is this processing a real position, or counting against
   something that does not exist? Deferred decisions, unfinished conversations, and
   promises I have not withdrawn are phantoms.
3. **Count against the window, not against ambition.** The number is smaller than I
   want. It has always been smaller than I want.
4. **Cut until the count fits.** Withdraw the promise out loud, make the decision
   badly and now, or let the thing disappear. Deferral is not a cut. Deferral is a
   core set with a nice name.
5. **Do not call step 4 a failure of will.** It is the only operation the container
   supports.

**The step this does not contain:** making the not-vital list *while empty*, on a
calm afternoon, by a version of me who will not be in the room when it fires. Apollo
did that years before the descent. I have never done it once.

## The basin check

*Is this a bad week or a bad regime?*

The boundary between compounding and diverging is not clean. It is **sensitive**.
A good week and a trash week can differ by one meeting at the wrong time, one
breach of sleep, one context switch I did not respect, one *I'll just do this
quickly* that eats an evening.

From far away that looks like discipline. Up close it looks like dynamics.

So do not ask *why am I like this*. Ask **what conditions keep me in the convergent
basin** — a question with a testable answer, where the first one is neither.

## The flat-text test

Ten seconds. Open something you were reading and ask whether the sentences have
any distance from each other.

- **Good loop:** you know what is adjacent, you know what constrains what, you can
  forget a detail and regenerate the path.
- **Full container:** everything is flat text. Every sentence is equally far away.
  Nothing is nearer than anything else, so nothing is structure, so you are reading
  and storing nothing.

The test does not tell you what is wrong. It tells you the container is full, which
is the thing you otherwise only learn from mood.
